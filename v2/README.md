# DotGP v2 — oval racing with learned drivers

Successor to the prototype in the parent folder. Phase 1 spike: physics + track model + drivers that learn raw control.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev          # viewer at http://localhost:5173  (URL options: ?warm=60&field=24&zoom=4&laps=50&pace=4&focusS=0)
npm test             # physics + contact tests
npm run evolve       # headless genetic algorithm (kept for comparison; stalls with honest walls)
npx tsx scripts/practice.ts --rounds 400   # headless ES practice (solo ghosts), saves runs/*.json
npx tsx scripts/practice.ts --traffic 1 --pairs 8 --seconds 60 --lr 0.01 --from <file>   # practice in traffic
npx tsx scripts/race.ts <file> --track short --laps 50 --copies 3 --pace 4   # headless race: contact, pits, crew chiefs
npx tsx scripts/chiefs.ts --apprentice 80 --rounds 200 --sigma 0.05 --lr 0.01   # crew chiefs: imitate a veteran, then learn by ES
npx tsx scripts/inspect.ts runs/<file>.json [--track short]   # how champions drive
npx tsx scripts/trace.ts runs/<file>.json <lineage> <track>   # step-by-step trace
npx tsx scripts/incidents.ts --tracks speedway,short --seeds 2 --field 24 --pace 4   # race benchmark: wrecks, tires, fuel, stops, mark coverage
npx tsx scripts/bench.ts A.json B.json --seeds 8   # the same races in parallel, with 95% ranges; compares A and B race by race
npx tsx scripts/class.ts --lesson cautions --in drivers.json --out trained.json --rounds 100   # a driving school class, headless
npx tsx scripts/lab.ts status   # the training lab (below)
```

## Physics in one paragraph

The driver requests an acceleration in its travel frame (x = along velocity, y = toward the infield), magnitude ≤ 1, where 1 demands 125% of current tire capacity. Tires deliver up to `μ · grip · normalLoad`; forward drive is further capped by `P / (m v)`; demanding more than capacity slides (less force, more wear, darker marks). Banking: the sim is the top-down projection of a car on a banked surface. The surface pushes along its normal with a load that comes from how hard the car is actually cornering, `normalLoad = (g cos(bank) + tires⊥ sin(bank)) / cos²(bank)` (last step's tire force across the track; `g cos(bank) + v²κ sin(bank)` when holding a lane), so diving down the banking loads the tires more and drifting up it less; its horizontal part, `normalLoad · sin(bank)`, pushes toward the infield for free, so at the neutral speed `v² = g tan(bank) / κ` the banking alone carries the car. Tires grip in the banked plane: cornering force lies across the slope and only `cos(bank)` of it is horizontal, so the cornering limit matches the textbook banked curve, `v² = gR (sin b + μ cos b) / (cos b − μ sin b)` (tested from 0° to 30°). Banking is worth ~39% of lap time on the speedway. The Debug → Banking view shows it as a heatmap with inside → outside angles, and Debug → Forces draws each car's banking push (amber) against its tire force (cyan). Tire wear is the energy lost to slip: utilisation² × load × speed, utilisation = force / (μ · normalLoad) with cornering force taken in the banked plane, weighted lateral 1.0 / braking 0.35 / drive 0.2. On a flat track that is just (force / μg)²; banking that loads the tires lets them make the same force with less slip, so banked tracks are easier on tires and, where the banking steepens toward the wall, the high line saves tires (on 14° → 28° banking it wears ~18% less than the middle line at the same lap time). Worn tires fade gently to the cliff (life 0.3, 88% cornering grip), then fall away fast to 50% when corded; braking and drive lose only half as much as cornering, so the friction circle becomes an ellipse and a worn car mostly stops turning.

## Track generation

A track is a `TrackLayout` (plain JSON, ~4 KB): the generator writes it, the runtime `Track` reads it, and physics, drivers, race control and the renderer only see its features (surface regions, walls, pit lane, infield, markings, decor), never the parameters that made it. Adding a track type means adding a generator, not touching the sim or the renderer.

- **Types** register with `registerTrackType({ id, variants, sample, build, validate })`. `sample(rng, variant)` picks parameters; `build(params, ctx)` lays the track out by composing the shared stages.
- **Seeds are stable**: each stage draws from its own random stream (`ctx.rng('stage')`), so adding a stage doesn't change existing seeds' layouts. The same type, seed and variant always give the same track.
- **Validation**: every layout gets generic checks (closure, pit lane order, boxes, garage/buildings staying on their side of the infield, roads clear of buildings) plus the type's own; errors make the generator retry with a derived seed, warnings are kept in `layout.notes`.
- **Presets**: Speedway, Short track and Flat track are fixed-parameter ovals (`presetTrack(id)`), what the drivers train on and the season visits. In the viewer, *New oval* and *New circuit* generate random tracks; `?track=oval:<seed>[:<variant>]` (short, intermediate, speedway, superspeedway) or `?track=circuit:<seed>[:club|gp]` opens a specific one.
- **Road circuits** (`circuit.ts`): at real scale (cars are 3 m across), so a longer track has more corners, not bigger ones. The shape starts as an organic polygon (points round an ellipse at random radii, a few pushed out into peninsulas, which make the right-handers and hairpins), sized in metres to the class (club 1.8-3.2 km, GP 4-6.5 km). Each vertex becomes a corner from a vocabulary (hairpin 13-24 m radius, slow 20-45, medium 40-90, fast 80-200, kink beyond), set by how sharp the vertex is and the track's character (technical, power, flowing), fitted to the straights either side with clothoid easing the way road designers fillet an alignment; chicanes go into long straights. The lap closes exactly: the end position is linear in the straight lengths, so the smallest weighted change to them cancels the gap. Run-off on the outside of each corner is deep enough to stop a car leaving at corner speed (gravel or asphalt), kerbs mark apexes and exits, grass verges run out to the barriers, and the pit lane and paddock sit on the left of the main straight (the loop runs anticlockwise, so that's the infield). Validation: no part of the circuit, barriers included, comes within reach of another. Circuits get practice and ghost sessions for now: races need standing starts and a safety car (the circuit rulebook), and the drivers can't turn right yet.
- **Mirror**: `mirrorLayout()` gives any track in mirror image (every left turn a right turn). The sim has no handedness: a scripted car laps a mirrored track in exactly the original's times (test). The drivers, trained only on ovals, wreck on mirrored tracks within a lap or two.
- **Surfaces**: track, apron, pit, garage, road, grass (45% grip, heavy drag, bumps), and for circuits kerbs (90% grip, ridges that unsettle), asphalt run-off and gravel traps (35% grip; drag from ploughing through the stones, ~0.7 g at speed but only ~0.1 g at walking pace, so a beached car can crawl out).
- **Width**: the racing surface has an inside and an outside edge, each constant or a profile along the lap, so tracks can widen through the turns (ovals widen to the outside: `turnWidening`), narrow, or widen on one side. Walls, surfaces, banking and the drivers' lane input all follow the local edges.
- **Extending**: new decoration = a `Decoration` kind plus its painter in the renderer; new surface or wall = layout data; new feature = a stage in `stages.ts`; new shape (tri-oval, paperclip, road course) = curvature sections or a new centerline kind.

## Track layout (ovals)

Counterclockwise, start/finish mid front straight. No inner wall: past the apron is grass (45% grip, heavy drag, bumps). Pit entry leaves the apron at turn 4 exit; the pit wall runs along the front straight just inside the racing surface (two-sided, solid ends); pit road behind it (limiter 22 m/s, boxes every 12 m); the exit narrows back to the apron, which is the blend lane through turns 1-2 onto the back straight. Progress only counts distance actually driven, so cutting the infield earns nothing.

## Teams

Drivers, spotters and crew chiefs are separate agents with names and careers; a team is a car with one of each, and any seat can be reassigned under Paddock: Teams. Spotters differ in how early they call traffic and wrecks (same radio scale, shorter range). Crew chiefs are either scripted veterans or learned (apprenticed to the veteran, then improved by ES in strategy races).


Each car is a team: crew chief (strategy: when to pit, tires/fuel/repairs, once a lap before turn 3), spotter (global tower view, fills the radio), driver (learned network). During a pit cycle a scripted maneuver drives from the call to the blend-lane exit; races use a pace multiplier on tire wear and fuel burn (4x puts the speedway fuel window at ~13 laps). Four tires take 14 s and fuelling (6 kg/s) runs alongside, so tires are a real cost: the veteran chief learns the wear rate and only takes tires that won't last the next run.

## Race control

Races start behind the pace car: the field forms up double file, runs a pace lap, the pace car pulls into pit road at turn 4 exit and the green flies when the leader crosses the line. A wreck (or a car stopped on track) brings out the caution: everyone switches to formation driving (scripted, like the pit maneuver), the pace car comes out from pit road ahead of the leader, and wreckers drive out along the apron, hook the wreck and tow it to the garage via the escape lane or pit road. Once the track is clear (and at least two caution laps have run) it's one to go; the pace car pulls off and the leader takes the field to the green. Pit exit merging waits for the spotter's clear call.

## Stewards and the caution order

Rules are data (`src/rules/rulebook.ts`, the oval rulebook first; other disciplines will be other rulebooks): offences, and what each usually costs by how bad it was. A race run to a rulebook has a stewards' desk (`src/rules/desk.ts`): **detection** (`detect.ts`) reports what the sim can measure (pit road speeding, crossing the blend line, passing below the line on the apron, avoidable contact by fault, and race control's caution cases), **stewards** (`stewards.ts`) judge it by the rulebook's tables, read through their personality (strictness, consistency), and the desk **enforces**: warnings add up to a drive-through; time is added to the race time; under caution a car can be sent to the back of the line; drive-throughs and stop-and-gos are served on pit road within three green laps (the team calls the car in) or it's the black flag. Every decision goes on the broadcast feed.

**Stewards review incidents, not moments.** Rules here mimic people reviewing evidence, so nothing is ruled at the instant something might have happened. Detection opens an incident, it gathers evidence until the situation has played out, and the stewards judge the outcome and how clear the evidence is; every incident goes "under review" on the broadcast feed and the decision ("no further action" included) comes after the review time (10 s). Passing below the line is a move: two cars alongside (less than a car length apart) is a possible pass; when they're no longer alongside, if the car that started behind came out ahead, the share of the move it made with all four wheels below the line is the evidence (under half: benefit of the doubt). The line rule applies on superspeedways only, as in NASCAR (none of the presets is one; generated superspeedway ovals are). A restart is judged as a whole: the cars in the line when the pace car pulls off, and who is physically ahead of whom, are the reference; at the green, a car a car length clear of one that was ahead of it in the line got by before the line. Cars still catching up aren't lined up, so passing them isn't gaining a place.

**The caution order** works as it does for real. When the caution comes out the running order is frozen (race distance at that moment); it changes only when cars pit (they rejoin at the back), retire, or are sent back. Race control compares it with the cars on track, between cars that are in the line (within a row of their place): a car ahead of one it should be behind is told over the radio to line up behind it, and drives out of the way (up into the yield lane) to let it by; still out of order a full lap after the call (calls open at the green carry on) it's the stewards' case. "Ahead" is judged the shorter way round between the two cars, not by race distance: a car a lap down running ahead of a lead-lap car is ahead of it. Cars running ahead of the leader when the caution comes out aren't in the line; they go round and join its tail (waved around). Once the pace car is off, passing a car ahead in line before the green is a jumped restart. Getting here took a few wrong turns, kept in mind for the formation driving work to come: grace periods and distance windows hid the real problem; race distance mixes laps; a frozen order without a way to give places back deadlocks; waiting for the field to form up holds the race hostage to any stuck interaction.

**Formation driving** (the script that drives cautions for drivers without the caution license, and teaches the Cautions lesson):
- *Two phases*, as for real. *Caution out*: there's no line yet. Each driver slows to caution pace when they react, holds their lane and position, and closes up on the car ahead in their own way. *The pace car picks up the leader*: it waits at the end of pit road and pulls out just in front of the leader as it arrives; cars on the road between them are waved around. From there the line forms, and one to go comes no sooner than a full lap behind the pace car.
- *No hive mind*: every driver has their own reaction to the caution and the green (0.3-1 s), following gap and eagerness to close up, shifted by nerve (bold drivers sit closer and close up harder). Until they react they carry on: racing into a caution, holding station into a green.
- *Single file under caution* in the official order, in the middle of the track, a car length and the following gap apart; *doubled up at one to go*, the leader on the inside and the order alternating (the start's pace laps are double file throughout).
- *Giving way is one rule*: the car that's out of place moves low (a car's width inside the line) and slows; the car that belongs ahead carries on in the line. That covers a car told to line up behind another, one too far forward, and one being caught by a car that belongs ahead of it (it moves over before that car has to brake).
- *Catching up*: faster than the pace car, braking smoothly to the tail; a car may get by cars behind it in the official order (restoring the order), never cars ahead of it.
- *Passing a wreck*: on the side you're already on, never across in front of it.
- *The restart*: green when the leader crosses the line; everyone accelerates, but no car may gain a place until it has crossed the line itself (judged per car as it crosses, against cars in earlier rows that haven't crossed yet).
Out of order means clear ahead by a car length; nose to nose is two cars settling into line. Short track, 4 races: cars in their place at the green 58% -> 74%, average distance from their place at one to go 97 -> 44 m. Over 8 races, formation pileups on the short track went 2.3 -> 6.9 (single file, everyone braking at once) -> 1.3 (two phases).

**Road circuit rules** (`CIRCUIT_RULES`; a session races to the rulebook for its track type). The rulebook carries procedures as well as offences:
- *Standing start*: every car in its own grid box behind the line, staggered. A formation lap single file in grid order behind a reference at formation pace (60% of each corner's limit, capped on straights), braking gently into the pole box; the field stops in its boxes and holds still on the brakes once the lights are on. Five red lights a second apart, a random 0.2-3 s hold, lights out: the race starts there, each driver launching on its own reaction (wheelspin and all), each car's first lap starting at the line. Waiting on the grid isn't stalling.
- *Safety car*: comes out of pit exit in front of the leader, as the pace car does, but drives the track at formation pace (quick on the straights, slow in the hairpins); the field stays single file throughout and restarts single file; each driver may drive the safety car's pace for where it is plus its own eagerness, so the line breathes like a real one (bunched in slow corners, stretched on straights) without anyone passing. Marshals with a crane lift a wreck off where it stopped (25 s); "safety car in this lap", into the pit entry, restart at the line.
- *Track limits*: every time the whole car is past the white line (its centre a car's half width beyond the edge), under green, counts once as it begins; pit lane and the pit approach don't. Race control counts them as they happen rather than the stewards reviewing each: up to the grace they're noted ("track limits at turn 4, 2 of 3"), the last with the black-and-white flag, and each one after that is 5 s. The grace (`trackLimits.strikes`, 3 here) is a league's to set: lenient for beginners, tight at the top. Corners are numbered from the start line, as on real circuits.
- Ovals keep the rolling start and the pace car (`OVAL_RULES`, no track limits: the apron has its own rule). Still to come for circuits: local yellows and the virtual safety car, blue flags, and drivers who know about track limits (an input, and penalties in learning).

## Infield

Garage lot behind pit road (wrecks are parked there), escape lane from the back-straight apron across the infield. Progress only counts distance driven, so neither is a shortcut.

## Roster, events and records

Sessions run as **events**, each in its own worker. One event is on screen (attached): it runs in real time × the viewing speed and streams snapshots and tire-mark strokes to the renderer. The rest run in the background: races flat out (a 10-lap race finishes in a second or two), open sessions paused, since they exist to be watched and their learning happens in the trainer. The Events list (Watch view) lists them with Watch / Background / Stop. When a race finishes in the background, the weekend loop carries on in the background (practice, then the next race), so a season can run unwatched. **Whose drivers.** Paddock: *Drivers on track* switches between your own roster (it grows, learns, races the championship, saved in the browser) and a bundle shipped with the game, listed in `public/drivers/index.json`. *House drivers* are `public/drivers/default.json`, exactly as the benchmarks and Claude's tests race them: each car an exact copy, cycling through the bundle, with scripted crew chiefs and perfect spotters. A bundle is read-only (no learning, classes, practice or points) and never saved over your roster; `?roster=house` opens with it (handy for sharing what a test saw), and the choice is remembered. Only championship rounds score points: the field races the calendar's next track (Series: *Race the next round*, or the weekend loop). Picking a track or session by hand under *Exhibition* runs an exhibition: recorded in everyone's history, no points.

Every agent (driver, spotter, crew chief) belongs to the organisation: seated in a team's car, or in the **academy** with no seat (released drivers land there; *Recruit* signs new ones). Whatever is on screen is an **event** (a race, a practice session, ghost laps) that claims its participants: an agent takes part in one event at a time, and may learn during any of them. Practice training runs only inside the practice session, so the field never practises and races at once.

Everything agents do goes into a permanent **activity log** (IndexedDB): arrivals, signings and releases, every practice round with best laps per track, licenses, crew chiefs' strategy rounds, race results (position, laps, best/average lap, stops, wall hits, points) and season standings, each tagged with the event and the agent's brain version. Click a name in the Paddock view's Roster (or double-click a driver in the timing tower) for their history page.

## Learning in races

On by default (*Learn* in the Series view's race settings). In a race each driver drives a small variation of itself for a lap and its opposite for another (which goes first is a coin toss, so tire wear and fuel burn can't fake a signal), and every three pairs steps toward the better direction. Only clean green-flag laps count: a lap with a caution, a pit stop or a wreck is run again with the same variation, and damage taken on a lap is charged 120 s per unit of health, so contact never pays. Learning is slow and steady (a few steps per driver per race; drivers improve their solo lap times ~0.5-1.5% over a dozen races in testing) and races stay as clean as without it. Each step reaches the roster as it happens and is logged in the driver's history; the HUD counts the steps taken this race.

**Fault.** Every car-to-car contact is judged on the velocities going in: closing on a car from behind is the follower's fault, moving across into a car is the mover's, and neither (touching side by side at the same speed) is shared. Each car keeps the damage it did to others times its share of the fault (`harmDone`), and the damage it did itself (walls, plus its share of contact: `selfInflicted`). Race learning charges a lap 120 s per unit of harm done, as for damage taken, and a lap the driver spoiled itself (it did itself or others harm) counts against its last clean lap time instead of being run again, even when it ends in a wreck: being wrecked by someone else still doesn't count. The caution and pit-traffic drills charge harm by fault too. The incidents benchmark reports contact retirements at fault vs hit by another, and the damage done by fault per race.

**Fear, trauma and nerve.** A real driver avoids risk for reasons that have nothing to do with the result: a big crash can end a career or a life. Drivers get some of that. *Fear* (`src/ai/fear.ts`) is a steady signal from moments of danger the driver makes itself: closing on the car ahead with under 1.2 s to contact, moving across into a car alongside or toward a wall with under 0.6 s. Crashes are rare, so learning from them alone is noise; near misses are constant. *Trauma* weighs each impact as the driver feels it: small knocks are nothing, big hits far worse than their size (`damage + 6·damage²`). Race learning charges a lap for seconds spent in danger and for trauma beyond the damage itself, scaled by the driver's *nerve*: its appetite for risk, 0 timid to 1 fearless (fear costs twice as much at 0, nothing at 1). Nerve is a personality trait: new drivers draw one, descendants inherit something of their mentor's, and the driver's page shows it. It doesn't change what a driver can do, only what it learns to care about. `npx tsx scripts/season.ts --nerve 0.5` runs a season with learning on (retirements, damage done, passes, lap time, danger per lap, race by race). First seasons: on the short track fear cut damage done to others race by race (16, 13, 8 against 22, 20, 19 without it) and passes by about 40%; nine races of race learning weren't enough to set it into the drivers, and it doesn't yet cut retirements.

**Injuries and careers.** A race's hardest single hit can hurt the driver, not just the car (`src/events/injury.ts`): nobody is hurt below 0.4 health lost in one hit; past that the chance rises (to 40% at most). Hits under 0.8 mean a minor injury (out 1-2 races), harder ones a serious one (3-6 races), and one in ten drivers hurt by a hit that destroys the car in one go never races again. Fault doesn't come into it. Injured drivers sit out races (counted in races run, since events only advance when run) while an academy driver stands in, and get their seat back once fit; a driver whose career is over keeps their history but races no more (no classes either). Injuries, recoveries and career endings are in the driver's history and the news. With today's drivers about two drivers in a 24-car race are hurt and a career ends every five or six races; cleaner drivers mean fewer. This is what makes nerve matter beyond one race's result.

**Racecraft (experimental).** A lesson for racing in a pack without wrecking anyone. What we learned getting it this far: drills scored only on crashes are noise (crashes are rare in 40 s), and pack drills alone let everything they don't measure drift. Drivers lost their way round the track, then got faster and hit harder, then got slower: one slow car in a 24-car field gets rear-ended all race. So drills are scored on fear too, every candidate also drives solo laps, and solo pace and cleanliness are held to the student's own from before the class (a moving reference let small losses through every round). Benchmarked after 80 rounds (retirements per 24-car race, against the bundled drivers): the speedway is clearly cleaner in every version (5.8 against 10.3 with the current one); the short track is worse (16 against 9.5), and the flat track is noisy. Part of the short track's count is the formation script's caution-line wrecks, which racecraft doesn't touch.

## Driving school

Pick a lesson and free drivers (academy or anyone not in a running session) in the Academy view's Driving school and start a class: it's an event like any other, on screen or in the background. Each round every student drives as a ghost alongside its slightly varied copies (32 in Rookie school, 16 in Track variety and Racecraft, 12 in Cautions) (faint dots in its colour; *Variants: Hide* to declutter) and then moves toward the copies that did better: the same evolution-strategies training as practice, one visible track per round. **Rookie school** rotates the three presets with soft walls hardening over the first 60 lessons; a student earns a license with a clean lap at full walls on each preset, and the class ends when everyone has one. **Track variety** puts licensed drivers on a new generated oval every round. Every round goes into each student's history (lesson, track, laps, best lap, how many variations beat them, wall softness). *Raw rookie* in the Paddock view's Roster signs an unlicensed driver for the academy.

## Drivers doing their own pit stops

**Withdrawn from the driving school (2026-10-16).** In the lab, both pit lessons cost drivers their racing: they train the whole network, and the outside guards didn't stop it (lap times 7-10 s slower, retirements doubled or worse). They come back as a separate pit-stop skill network (see PLAN.md); the lab can still run them. What follows describes how they worked.

The goal is no scripted driving: the pit maneuver and formation laps are scripts only until drivers learn them. Drivers see the pit stop in progress as network inputs (in-lap / out-lap, where the painted pit route runs at 0-100 m ahead, distance to the box, the speed limit zone and how far off the limit they are, the spotter's clear-to-merge), added with zero weights so racing is unchanged. The **Pit stops** lesson (Driving school, for licensed drivers) first has each student copy the scripted maneuver (DAgger: watch the script, then drive under its eye and learn its answers from wherever your own mistakes put you; a sample of the student's original racing is kept so learning to pit doesn't overwrite how it races), then practise: ghost pit stops scored on distance covered, with credit for getting onto pit road and slowing at the box before ever completing a stop, a drive-through's worth of penalty for speeding, and a penalty for not merging back. A clean stop on every preset earns the pit license, and from then on the driver drives its own stops in races. Students that stop making progress (no stop completed for a while) go back for a refresher with the script; three of the four bundled drivers earn the pit license within ~150 rounds.

The pit license is solo; races are not. **Pit road in traffic** (for drivers with a pit license) puts the stop in a race: two cars just ahead pit too (the veteran's maneuver), so there's a queue on pit road, and four more race around the student, two closing from behind as it slows. The racers drive as the student races, so it's a race's traffic. Students first go over the veteran's stop in traffic (DAgger, three starting lanes per preset), then practise; a student that settles into skipping the stop goes back for a refresher. The license takes a stop in traffic on every preset that is completed, unhurt, hurts nobody, never speeds, and scores at least as well as the veteran's maneuver driving the same drill. Drivers need both licenses to do their own stops in races. Two bundled drivers both earned it within 33 rounds (~5 minutes headless). Still scripted: pit stops for drivers without both licenses.

## The lab: training in the background

Training runs as a background job beside feature work (see `PLAN.md`). The lab is a second checkout of the repo (`../<repo>-lab`, made by `lab.ts init`) pinned to a commit, so classes and benchmarks there are never disturbed by edits here; `lab.ts sync` moves it to this checkout's HEAD (only committed code goes in). Experiments are JSON specs in `lab/queue/` (git ignores `lab/`): steps run in order, `class` (a driving school class), `season` (races with learning on), `bench` (house vs candidate, 8 races a track by default, on 6 cores) and `promote`. `lab.ts run --once` works through the queue; each experiment gets a results folder (spec, step logs, the trained drivers, `bench.json`) and a line in `lab/RESULTS.md`. The **house drivers** (`lab/house.json`, seeded from the bundled ones) are what experiments start from; `promote` replaces them only if the benchmark says the candidate is no worse on any track (retirements, lap time) and better on at least one (retirements), keeping the old ones in `lab/house-history/`. Promoted house drivers reach the game by copying them to `public/drivers/default.json` in a commit.

## Lessons must not cost drivers their racing

Every lesson measures one skill, and a driver learning it is free to change everything else. Twice that went badly: Racecraft drivers drifted slower lap after lap, and a Cautions class licensed drivers that couldn't finish a solo lap (a field of them lost 23 of 24 cars a race). Three guards now apply to every drill lesson (`src/school/drill.ts`, `src/school/imitation.ts`):

- **Apprenticeships keep the racing in.** Each round trains on as many moments of the student's own original racing (solo laps on every preset) as of the skill being taught; caution moments are sampled rather than all recorded.
- **Practice holds solo pace.** Every round, each variation also drives 60 s of solo laps next to the student as it drove before the class, and is charged for distance and cleanliness lost against that (never rewarded for gaining). Distance, not laps completed: in 60 s most drivers complete exactly one lap, fast or slow, and a check on whole laps let 10% of pace through unseen.
- **Licenses check racing.** A license also needs clean solo laps within 3% of the pre-class pace on the track it is earned on, and a student who has passed stops learning in that lesson while the class catches up.

**The Cautions lesson trains only its own weights.** The guards above check racing from outside, and they missed traffic: solo laps stayed within 3% while racing in packs got worse (on the flat track, green-flag wrecks went from 8 to 21 in six races). The caution inputs are zero under green, so the lesson now trains only the first-layer weights from those inputs (`inputWeightMask`, in the apprenticeship and in practice, weight decay included): racing is untouched by construction, solo laps identical to the metre. The caution drills also come in two kinds: the field bunched after a start, and strung out round the lap (a race's cautions find cars half a lap from their place in line, and drivers trained only on bunched cautions charged the line). Place error is capped at 30 m a moment, so catching up fast isn't worth more than not hitting anyone. With all four bundled drivers driving their own cautions (only one licensed), the flat track is now within the noise of the formation script (7 retirements a race against 5; it was 15); the short track and speedway still lose more (15 and 17 against 12 and 11), with fewer cautions and less damage done by fault. Half of each practice pack is now classmates driving their own cautions (the other half the calm scripted pack), as in a race where everyone around a student drives theirs too; the license baseline uses the same pack. Three of four bundled drivers earned the license in 100 rounds (one in 60 before), and with only them driving their own cautions: speedway 13.5 and flat 6.6 retirements a race, within the noise of the formation script (10.9, 4.9), no formation pileups; the short track is still worse (15.4 against 11.6). The pit lessons can't use the same masking: trained on the pit inputs' weights alone, students mostly missed the pit road (unmasked, nearly all made clean stops), so they keep the outside guards.

## Drivers doing their own cautions

Same plan for formation laps, cautions and restarts. Drivers see a caution as more network inputs (pacing, restart coming, how far ahead or behind their place in the line is, which lane it's in, the line's speed, the gap and closing speed to the car ahead in their file), zero under green and added with zero weights. The **Cautions** lesson (for licensed drivers) runs drills in an 8-car field: a short green, a caution for debris, a caution lap, the restart, a short green. The rest of the pack is calm and scripted so the drill tests the student, not the pack. Each student first copies the formation script (DAgger, keeping a sample of its own green-flag racing), then practises: every variation drives its own drill from the same grid slot, scored on green-flag distance minus place and lane error under caution, passes under caution and damage done to others. The license takes a drill on every preset with no damage taken or done that scores at least as well as the formation script driving the same drill; from then on the driver drives its own cautions and restarts in races. Two bundled drivers both earned it within 45 rounds (~8 minutes headless). When the pace car isn't leading, the line follows a virtual pace car moving at pace speed from where the leader is (set from the leader itself, the line could wait on itself: cars ahead of their slots slowed for them, the leader stuck behind them slowed too, and the slots stopped). Under pacing, cars only swap places in line once one is clearly (8 m) ahead: re-sorting by distance every step swapped cars side by side in a row, and their files with them. (Freezing the order outright was worse: a car that really had got ahead had to drop back through a crawling pack, and the field gridlocked.)

## Rookie school

Brand-new drivers (random wiring, plus an instinct to accelerate and no attention to the radio yet) can't drive, and traffic practice teaches them nothing. Until they hold a license they practise solo as ghosts, with soft walls that harden over their own first 60 lessons, a damage penalty that starts gentle (so driving and crashing beats sitting still) and ramps to the race value over the next 60 lessons (learn to drive, then learn to drive clean), 16 variation pairs and 80 s trials, and they get first call on practice slots. They earn the license by lapping every track cleanly (90%+ health) at full damage, then practise in traffic like everyone else. Drivers descended from a trained mentor are licensed from the start. `npx tsx scripts/rookies.ts --field 8` checks how fast a fresh field graduates.
