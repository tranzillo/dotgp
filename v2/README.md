# DotGP v2 — oval racing with learned drivers

Successor to the prototype in the parent folder. Phase 1 spike: physics + track model + drivers that learn raw control.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev          # viewer at http://localhost:5173  (URL options: ?warm=60&field=24&zoom=4&laps=50&pace=4&focusS=0)
npm test             # physics + contact tests
npm run evolve       # headless genetic algorithm (kept for comparison; stalls with honest walls)
npx tsx scripts/practice.ts --rounds 400   # headless ES practice (solo ghosts), saves runs/*.json
npx tsx scripts/practice.ts --traffic 1 --pairs 8 --seconds 60 --lr 0.01 --from <file>   # practice in traffic
npx tsx scripts/race.ts <file> --track short --laps 50 --copies 3 --pace 4   # headless race: contact, pits, crew chiefs
npx tsx scripts/chiefs.ts --apprentice 80 --rounds 200 --sigma 0.05 --lr 0.01   # crew chiefs: imitate a veteran, then learn by ES
npx tsx scripts/strategy.ts bench --a caution --b veteran   # crew chiefs head to head in real races (paired, 95% ranges)
npx tsx scripts/strategy.ts train --from public/chiefs/default.json --rounds 100   # crew chiefs learn race strategy in real races
npx tsx scripts/inspect.ts runs/<file>.json [--track short]   # how champions drive
npx tsx scripts/trace.ts runs/<file>.json <lineage> <track>   # step-by-step trace
npx tsx scripts/incidents.ts --tracks speedway,short --seeds 2 --field 24 --pace 4   # race benchmark: wrecks, tires, fuel, stops, mark coverage
npx tsx scripts/bench.ts A.json B.json --seeds 8   # the same races in parallel, with 95% ranges; compares A and B race by race
npx tsx scripts/class.ts --lesson cautions --in drivers.json --out trained.json --rounds 100   # a driving school class, headless (--jobs N threads; same results as 1)
npx tsx scripts/circuits.ts A.json B.json --tracks 6 --seeds 3   # circuit races on unseen circuits (circuit rulebook), A vs B race by race
npx tsx scripts/lab.ts status   # the training lab (below)
```

Viewer controls sit in the bar under the track: the race timeline (progress by the leader, cautions shaded, markers for wrecks, stewards' decisions and lead changes; hover for what happened, click to watch it from 5 s before, drag to scrub), step back, play backwards, pause, step ahead, LIVE, speed (0.25× to 64×), camera, and Display (car size, trails, marks, debug layers). Rewinding the race on screen pauses it until the playhead is back at live (a replay that catches up goes live by itself); the tower, HUD, radio and tire marks follow the playhead. Keys: Space or K pause, J / L slower / faster, R play backwards, ← / → 5 s (Shift: 30 s), `,` / `.` step 0.1 s, Home / End the start / live, Z cycle the camera. Sessions keep a timeline of everything that happens (`Session.timeline`) and a tape (below), both sent to the page with the frames. Finished races are saved as replays, and driving-school rounds that were watched or earned a license; Settings → Replays lists them (watch, pin to keep, delete) and sets how much to keep (100 MB to 1 GB, 250 by default; past it the oldest unpinned go). Agents' history links to them while they're kept: a race result opens the race, an injury the hit, a learning step the lap, a lesson or license its round (moments open 5 s before, at 1×).

## Physics in one paragraph

The driver requests an acceleration in its travel frame (x = along velocity, y = toward the infield), magnitude ≤ 1, where 1 demands 125% of current tire capacity. Tires deliver up to `μ · grip · normalLoad`; forward drive is further capped by `P / (m v)`; demanding more than capacity slides (less force, more wear, darker marks). Banking: the sim is the top-down projection of a car on a banked surface. The surface pushes along its normal with a load that comes from how hard the car is actually cornering, `normalLoad = (g cos(bank) + tires⊥ sin(bank)) / cos²(bank)` (last step's tire force across the track; `g cos(bank) + v²κ sin(bank)` when holding a lane), so diving down the banking loads the tires more and drifting up it less; its horizontal part, `normalLoad · sin(bank)`, pushes toward the infield for free, so at the neutral speed `v² = g tan(bank) / κ` the banking alone carries the car. Tires grip in the banked plane: cornering force lies across the slope and only `cos(bank)` of it is horizontal, so the cornering limit matches the textbook banked curve, `v² = gR (sin b + μ cos b) / (cos b − μ sin b)` (tested from 0° to 30°). Banking is worth ~39% of lap time on the speedway. The Banking debug layer (Display, in the control bar) shows it as a heatmap with inside → outside angles, and the Forces layer draws each car's banking push (amber) against its tire force (cyan). Tire wear is the energy lost to slip: utilisation² × load × speed, utilisation = force / (μ · normalLoad) with cornering force taken in the banked plane, weighted lateral 1.0 / braking 0.35 / drive 0.2. On a flat track that is just (force / μg)²; banking that loads the tires lets them make the same force with less slip, so banked tracks are easier on tires and, where the banking steepens toward the wall, the high line saves tires (on 14° → 28° banking it wears ~18% less than the middle line at the same lap time). Worn tires fade gently to the cliff (life 0.3, 88% cornering grip), then fall away fast to 50% when corded; braking and drive lose only half as much as cornering, so the friction circle becomes an ellipse and a worn car mostly stops turning.

## Track generation

A track is a `TrackLayout` (plain JSON, ~4 KB): the generator writes it, the runtime `Track` reads it, and physics, drivers, race control and the renderer only see its features (surface regions, walls, pit lane, infield, markings, decor), never the parameters that made it. Adding a track type means adding a generator, not touching the sim or the renderer.

- **Types** register with `registerTrackType({ id, variants, sample, build, validate })`. `sample(rng, variant)` picks parameters; `build(params, ctx)` lays the track out by composing the shared stages.
- **Seeds are stable**: each stage draws from its own random stream (`ctx.rng('stage')`), so adding a stage doesn't change existing seeds' layouts. The same type, seed and variant always give the same track.
- **Validation**: every layout gets generic checks (closure, pit lane order, boxes, garage/buildings staying on their side of the infield, roads clear of buildings) plus the type's own; errors make the generator retry with a derived seed, warnings are kept in `layout.notes`.
- **Presets**: Speedway, Short track and Flat track are fixed-parameter classic ovals (`presetTrack(id)`), what the drivers train on and the season visits. A test pins their layouts byte for byte: change them only on purpose. In the viewer, Series → *Generate a venue* makes random ones (kind, size class, oval shape) and adds them to the Exhibition list without starting anything; `?track=oval:<seed>[:<variant>[:<shape>]]` (short, intermediate, speedway, superspeedway; classic, egg, tri-oval, d-oval, quad-oval, rectangle, triangle) or `?track=circuit:<seed>[:club|gp]` opens a specific one.
- **Ovals** (`oval.ts`): a closed loop of left turns joined by straights, 360 degrees in all, each corner an arc eased in and out by clothoids with its own radius, easing, banking and widening. One builder makes every shape: the classic oval and paperclip; the egg (ends of different radii, the straights closing in toward the tight end); the tri-oval (a long sweeping bend in the front stretch, the line at its apex) and the D (a big bend and hardly any straight either side); the quad-oval (two bends, the line on the short straight between them); the rectangle (four 90-degree corners, tighter than a classic oval's turns, and short chutes); and the triangle (three corners of different angles and radii, the longest side the front stretch). Each size class has its own mix of shapes (superspeedways are mostly tri-ovals, short tracks mostly classic and egg). The shape gives target lengths; the lap then closes exactly, as for circuits, by the smallest weighted change to the straights. Banking follows each corner's design speed: corners tighter than the track's typical turn bank steeper, sweeping ones less (a tri-oval bend about 60% of the turns, as at Daytona), and shapes built for flat corners (rectangle, triangle) bank less overall; up to 36 degrees. The straights away from the line are sometimes narrower or wider than the front stretch. Pit road runs along the front stretch (from the last turn to turn 1, bends included; the entry taper shortens where the last turn is a short 90-degree corner), and the escape lane leaves the longest other straight.
- **Venues, layouts, series** (`src/venue/`, `src/rules/series.ts`): making a track and racing on it are separate. A venue has one or more layouts, each a whole lap with a kind (superspeedway, speedway, short oval, dirt oval, road: `layoutKind`); a series races on some kinds, to its rulebook; a race is a series on one of a venue's layouts. The presets and generated tracks are venues with one layout. Drivers bring their road-course skill to any road layout.
- **Infield road network** (`layout.infield.network`, `sim/roads.ts`): every road off the racing surface as a graph of nodes (apron accesses with their s, junctions, garage nodes) and roads (world polylines with a width and a grade: escape, service, pit, garage, course; some one-way). Wreckers tow along it: off the oval at the nearest access that leads to a garage, then the shortest way there. Layouts without a network (the presets, saved replays) get one built from their pit road and escape lanes, exactly the routes wreckers always took. Validation: roads join their nodes, every way off the oval leads to a garage.
- **Pit complexes** (generated ovals, `PitPlan`, `stages.pitComplex`): about a third of pit roads are set back across a grass island (4-45 m; less on short tracks), reached by paved lanes that swing over from the apron at the entry and back at the exit. The layout lays these out (`pit.entryRoute` / `exitRoute`) and everyone follows them through `pitPath` (ai/pitting.ts): the cars' pit autopilot, the pace car, the learned drivers' pit route; the lanes carry their own speed limit from how sharply they swing across (3.5 m/s^2 sideways), and the line follower matches their sideways slope. A set-back pit road swings over on the front stretch, not round a turn, so its wall moves down the straight to make room, the gap shrinks where that leaves under 12 boxes, and where nothing fits pit road goes against the track. Entry, exit and blend lengths, wall insets and box spacing vary too. The garage sits right behind pit road or set back across grass, reached by one to three garage lanes. Every other pit lesson round is on a set-back pit road; a driver that has made a clean stop on one does its own stops there in races (`setBackPits`), others leave those to the maneuver. `scripts/pitsurvey.ts`: the scripted pit cycle on 100 generated ovals (100 clean, 35 set back).
- **Generated infields** (`infield.ts`, `InfieldPlan` in a generated oval's parameters; placement drawn from the build's own random stream, so a seed always gives the same infield): a **service road** round the infield, off the pavement's edge by a sampled offset and further in through the turns (smoothed: a running maximum then a running mean, so it doesn't follow every taper of pit road in and out), held back from the infield's middle where it narrows, and round the back of the garage lot where there's room (else a C ending at the lot's two ends). **Escape roads** (2-5 planned; at least one, short tracks rarely fit more beside pit road) leave the apron before and after the turns and along long straights, never in pit road's zone, and join the service road. **Links** straight across the infield (0-2, none round an inner oval), **parking lots** with painted bays (0-3, bigger at bigger tracks), a **skidpad** (a paved disc, as at Iowa and Pikes Peak) on about a third of the bigger tracks, and an **inner oval** (a bullring or short track with its own pit road and garage) on a quarter to a half of intermediate-and-up tracks: placed in the middle, out in either end turned across it (Las Vegas's Legends oval), or toward the front or back (Pocono's), the largest that fits keeping 20 m of grass from everything. A track with an inner oval is a venue of two layouts (`venueLayouts`); in the viewer the outer one goes by its kind (Speedway, Superspeedway). `scripts/infieldsurvey.ts`: 100 generated ovals, all laid out and lapped cleanly, every way off the oval reaching a garage; 41 with inner ovals. Reference infields the user collected are in `feedback/infield layouts/` (Daytona, Iowa, Las Vegas, Pikes Peak, Pocono).
- **Road courses on ovals (rovals)** (`roval.ts`, rebuilt 2026-10-18; replaces the course roads and racing network): on about three in four generated intermediate-and-up ovals the infield's road course comes first (the services, placeholders for now, stay out where it goes in). The oval's part of the lap is a straight, a banked turn, or both (often all of it but one end, as at Daytona and Kansas). The course leaves it partway round a turn or along a straight, follows the turn at a constant offset to where the oval runs square to the front stretch, then winds through the infield the way real infield courses do: legs along and across the front stretch, square corners, fingers and hooks (two legs 40 to 90 m apart and a hairpin), the walk chosen from many for its hairpins and long legs, never a staircase, and closed exactly back onto the oval. Corners come from the circuit fitting (eased, concentric with the oval's turns where the course follows them). Its cross-section is circuit.ts's (`dressCourse`, with a `Course` and a `Bound` for the oval's pavement) laid as an open infield: 11 to 14 m wide, kerbs, no continuous barriers or run-off, concrete only beside the oval's pavement, tyre walls and a little gravel at hairpins after long legs. `compileRoval` makes it one closed lap (banking and width easing over 35 m where it meets the oval, the start line pinned to the oval's or to its own pit straight's), with barriers closing the oval where the course leaves and rejoins it. Pits are a placeholder: the oval's pit road if the oval's part takes it in, else a pit lane on one of the course's straights. `validateOval` compiles and validates every roval; `infieldsurvey.ts` laps them all. See PLAN.md (Track G) for where this stands.
- **Foundations for shared racing surfaces** (redesign R1): `polygon` surface regions (world rings, even-odd so inner rings are grass holes; point tests through a band-bucketed edge index, built on first use); course roads that leave or join the oval **mid-turn**, easing in from (or out to) the turn's own curvature so the lap's curvature is unbroken, with the roval's banking, width and paved edge easing from the oval's to the course road's over 35 m at each end (headings at a junction are interpolated between centerline samples: in a turn the sample before is off by up to a sample's turn, a metre over a course road); **every layout's lines painted**, the raced layout's bright and the rest (scenery tracks' paint and course roads' edges) faint, walls at full strength whichever is raced; and road networks with **paved areas** (`network.areas`) that wreckers cross straight between any two nodes on them, where the line stays on the pavement.
- **Chord pit roads** (redesign R2): on a front stretch that bends (tri-oval, quad-oval), about half the time pit road runs straight across it, from where the last turn ends to where turn 1 begins, as at Daytona, Kansas and Iowa: the oval races the bulge, the grass between is the tri-oval's infield, the garage stands behind pit road where it is furthest in. The pit lane carries an **offset profile** (`pit.offset`, `Track.pitOffset(s)`): how much further in the whole pit complex (wall, road, lanes, boxes) stands at each s, rounded over 80 m where the chord leaves the track at an angle. Every pit consumer follows it (the autopilot, drivers' pit routes, the pace car, race control, the missed-pit rule, surfaces, walls and paint); the lanes in and out are laid as blends that arrive along pit road's line, not just at it; the lane speed limits run through to the wall's ends; cars are called in 700 m before a laid pit lane (300 m otherwise), since they have to be slow before the swing; braking is planned more gently on a lane that swings across the track; and a chord's fast lane is 1.5 m further from the wall. `pitsurvey.ts`: 100 clean stops in 100 generated ovals, 16 of them on chords.
- **Complexes** (`complex.ts`): several rings placed in one frame (`layout.placement`: the centred centerline turned and moved; `placeLayout` moves the layout's world positions with it). Each ring is a layout of its own, raced alone, and carries the venue's other rings as `layout.neighbours`: scenery drawn round it (outermost first, each painting over the infield of the one round it), drivable (their pavement where this track has only grass; flat, their banking isn't modelled here) and solid (their walls stop cars from either side, measured from their own centerlines through a grid index, `Centerline.near`). At a venue the racing surface no longer runs on open past the wall. Rings that hold another track reach their garage by a service road round the edge of their infield instead of across it. `validateVenue` keeps 20 m of grass between any ring's features (outer wall, infield edge, garage, roads) and another's. The default **Mega Complex**: a 4.9 km tri-oval superspeedway banked 33 degrees (18 in the tri-oval), an Indy-style 2.6 km rectangle speedway in its infield (12 degrees), a Bristol-style 0.96 km short-track bowl inside that (24-28 degrees), and a 0.67 km bullring alone in the turn 3-4 end (the future dirt track). Stock cars race all four; the short track series the bowl and the bullring. It's the Exhibition panel's default venue; the championship still visits the presets, which the drivers train on.
- **Road circuits** (`circuit.ts`): at real scale (cars are 3 m across), so a longer track has more corners, not bigger ones. The shape starts as an organic polygon (points round an ellipse at random radii, a few pushed out into peninsulas, which make the right-handers and hairpins), sized in metres to the class (club 1.8-3.2 km, GP 4-6.5 km). Each vertex becomes a corner from a vocabulary (hairpin 13-24 m radius, slow 20-45, medium 40-90, fast 80-200, kink beyond), set by how sharp the vertex is and the track's character (technical, power, flowing), fitted to the straights either side with clothoid easing the way road designers fillet an alignment; chicanes go into long straights. The lap closes exactly: the end position is linear in the straight lengths, so the smallest weighted change to them cancels the gap. The cross-section follows the corners, giving drivers room where real circuits do. Braking hard into a slow corner off a long straight, the track opens into a funnel on the outside, widest at turn-in and narrowing to the apex (room for more than one line in), with a little extra on the inside for a car diving down it, and deep run-off straight on from the braking zone; out of a slow corner onto a long straight, the outside opens at the track-out and narrows back down the straight. Fast corners keep their width but get run-off round the outside and past the exit, deep enough to stop a car leaving at corner speed (less where they barely turn). Not every corner gets run-off: a slow one off a short straight may have grass to the barrier. The inside barrier of a tight corner runs parallel to the track all the way round (some hairpins right behind the apex kerb, others a little way back: far in, near the corner's centre, a barrier parallel to the track turns too tightly to look right), so its tip is a smooth curve, and eases out gently along the legs. Run-off (gravel or asphalt) fills to the barrier and tapers out of the verge either side, carrying across short gaps between corners. Chicanes leave room for a car that misses them to carry on: the barriers flow round the chicane's swing in one wide, smooth bulge (out at the verge from its furthest point, easing back to the straight's verge well before and after), with open asphalt between them and the track. Barriers move in or out by at most 0.4 m per metre along themselves and are smoothed over 12 m either side, both measured on the ground (a metre of centerline is less barrier round a corner's inside than its outside), so there are long tapers and no short jagged stretches; the limits they keep to by choice (tight round a corner's inside) are smoothed the same way, so where one gives way to another the barrier curves rather than turning a corner. Then the circuit is made safe for cars leaving it: from every point of each edge, a car carries on in the direction it was going (and turned out a little, or a lot) at roughly the speed it had there. A barrier it would run along is fine; one standing steeply across the way it was going needs room before it, more the faster and the steeper (two seconds' worth, up to 70 m). Where there isn't enough the barrier stands back, and the room gained is paved and painted (red and blue bands): the inside of a hairpin at the end of a long straight, say, opens out instead of a barrier tight round the apex, which is kept for hairpins nobody arrives at fast. Kerbs (blocks the same 3 m along the kerb however tight the corner, with plain white ends tapering into the track edge) mark apexes and exits, grass verges run out to the barriers, and the pit lane and paddock sit on the left of the main straight (the loop runs anticlockwise, so that's the infield). Nothing is laid out past the line midway to another part of the circuit (or a tight corner's centre): for each point of the centerline, the largest circle either side that holds no other part of it says how far that side can reach, and barriers stand short of it, so the inside barriers of a hairpin's two legs end in a rounded tip between them rather than crossing. Validation: every track edge keeps 4 m from that line, corners keep most of their run-off, and the pit lane and paddock keep theirs. Circuits get practice and ghost sessions for now: races need standing starts and a safety car (the circuit rulebook), and the drivers can't turn right yet.
- **Mirror**: `mirrorLayout()` gives any track in mirror image (every left turn a right turn). The sim has no handedness: a scripted car laps a mirrored track in exactly the original's times (test). The drivers, trained only on ovals, wreck on mirrored tracks within a lap or two.
- **Surfaces**: track, apron, pit, garage, road, grass (45% grip, heavy drag, bumps), and for circuits kerbs (90% grip, ridges that unsettle), asphalt run-off (dusty: 80% grip and some drag, so running wide is safe but not free) and gravel traps (35% grip; drag from ploughing through the stones, ~0.7 g at speed but only ~0.1 g at walking pace, so a beached car can crawl out).
- **Width**: the racing surface has an inside and an outside edge, each constant or a profile along the lap, so tracks can widen through the turns (ovals widen to the outside: `turnWidening`), narrow, or widen on one side. Walls, surfaces, banking and the drivers' lane input all follow the local edges.
- **Extending**: new decoration = a `Decoration` kind plus its painter in the renderer; new surface or wall = layout data; new feature = a stage in `stages.ts`; new shape (tri-oval, paperclip, road course) = curvature sections or a new centerline kind.

## Track layout (ovals)

Counterclockwise, start/finish mid front straight. No inner wall: past the apron is grass (45% grip, heavy drag, bumps). Pit entry leaves the apron at turn 4 exit; the pit wall runs along the front straight just inside the racing surface (two-sided, solid ends); pit road behind it (limiter 22 m/s, boxes every 12 m); the exit narrows back to the apron, which is the blend lane through turns 1-2 onto the back straight. Progress only counts distance actually driven, so cutting the infield earns nothing.

## Teams

Drivers, spotters and crew chiefs are separate agents with names and careers; a team is a car with one of each, and any seat can be reassigned under Paddock: Teams. Spotters differ in how early they call traffic and wrecks (same radio scale, shorter range). Crew chiefs are either scripted veterans or learned (apprenticed to the veteran, then improved by ES in strategy races). A learned chief sees its own stint (fuel and burn, tire life and wear, health, laps left, pace) and the race around it: the flag (caution, one to go, safety car), its place and laps down, the gaps either side, how old the tires either side are against its own, how many cars ahead are pitting now, and how long a caution has run. Veterans have temperaments, and some take a stop that's due anyway under caution (`cautionStop`). See PLAN.md (Track F) for where chiefs are going.


Each car is a team: crew chief (strategy: when to pit, tires/fuel/repairs, once a lap before turn 3), spotter (global tower view, fills the radio), driver (learned network). During a pit cycle a scripted maneuver drives from the call to the blend-lane exit; races use a pace multiplier on tire wear and fuel burn (4x puts the speedway fuel window at ~13 laps). Four tires take 14 s and fuelling (6 kg/s) runs alongside, so tires are a real cost: the veteran chief learns the wear rate and only takes tires that won't last the next run.

## Race control

Races start behind the pace car on its last pace lap (watchers needn't sit through the rest): the field double file through turns 3 and 4, the pace car pulling into pit road at turn 4 exit, and the green flies the first time the leader crosses the line. A wreck (or a car stopped on track) brings out the caution: everyone switches to formation driving (scripted, like the pit maneuver), the pace car comes out from pit road ahead of the leader, and wreckers drive out along the apron, hook the wreck and tow it to the garage via the escape lane or pit road. Once the track is clear (and at least two caution laps have run) it's one to go; the pace car pulls off and the leader takes the field to the green. Pit exit merging waits for the spotter's clear call.

## Qualifying

A session kind of its own (Series: Exhibition, *Qualifying*: fastest lap wins) and the middle of a race weekend: practice, qualifying, then the race, gridded in qualifying order (pole on the inside of the front row). The format is the rulebook's (`qualifying` in `rulebook.ts`): ovals qualify solo, circuits in a group; Race settings can pick one format everywhere, and set the solo laps (1 by default), the group's rounds (3) and their length (6 min). Every car waits in its pit box, out of the physics (`car.waiting`: nobody drives it, nothing touches it, it gives no draft), and is sent out from there. Its laps are timed from the line after it merges off pit road, so the out-lap never counts whichever side of the line the box is, and laps past the track limits don't count. When it's done the crew (`RunChief`) calls it in and the usual pit cycle (the maneuver, or the driver's own stop) brings it back to its box. A wreck ends a car's run (it keeps any time it set); so does a car that has made no progress on track for 15 s (beached, stuck, crawling through the gravel), or a run far past its time. No race pace in qualifying.

- **Solo** (oval style): in the run order (the draw), one car at a time runs an out-lap and its timed laps; as it starts its in-lap the next car leaves its box, so two cars are out at most and only one is ever on a timed lap. Classified by best lap. 24 cars, one lap each: 27-43 minutes of sim time on the presets (5-8 s flat out in the background).
- **Group** (F1 style): rounds against the clock with everyone still in it on track together (they leave the pits 1.5 s apart, on a fresh set and a full tank). A lap begun before the clock runs out still counts; then each car comes in at its next chance. After every round but the last the slowest go out: the starting field over the number of rounds each time (2 rounds: half, 3: a third...; 24 cars, 3 rounds: 24, 16, 8). The last round decides the front; everyone else is classified by their time in the round they went out in. The tower draws the cut line (the drop zone below it) and marks who went out in which round; the HUD and timeline show the round and its clock. 24 cars, 3 x 6 min: 21-35 minutes of sim time.

The tower, HUD and timeline read the session's qualifying view (round, phase, clock, cut, progress), which goes on tape in the frame header's race fields (a qualifying session has no race), so replays show the same. Each driver's result goes in their history (poles counted on their page); qualifying is kept as a replay like a race.

What the drivers make of it: solo runs are clean (they drive alone). In a group they race in traffic with no cautions and no respawns, and today's drivers scrape walls in packs as they do in practice (where wrecks respawn and hide it): on the short and flat ovals most of a 24-car field wrecks in Q1; the speedway is clean. On circuits the road apprentices also run wide, and laps past the white line don't count, so many cars set no time. Ovals qualify solo by default; group qualifying on circuits will be as good as the drivers' circuit racecraft.

## Stewards and the caution order

Rules are data (`src/rules/rulebook.ts`, the oval rulebook first; other disciplines will be other rulebooks): offences, and what each usually costs by how bad it was. A race run to a rulebook has a stewards' desk (`src/rules/desk.ts`): **detection** (`detect.ts`) reports what the sim can measure (pit road speeding, crossing the blend line, passing below the line on the apron, avoidable contact by fault, and race control's caution cases), **stewards** (`stewards.ts`) judge it by the rulebook's tables, read through their personality (strictness, consistency), and the desk **enforces**: warnings add up to a drive-through; time is added to the race time; under caution a car can be sent to the back of the line; drive-throughs and stop-and-gos are served on pit road within three green laps (the team calls the car in) or it's the black flag. Every decision goes on the broadcast feed.

**Stewards review incidents, not moments.** Rules here mimic people reviewing evidence, so nothing is ruled at the instant something might have happened. Detection opens an incident, it gathers evidence until the situation has played out, and the stewards judge the outcome and how clear the evidence is; every incident goes "under review" on the broadcast feed and the decision ("no further action" included) comes after the review time (10 s). Passing below the line is a move: two cars alongside (less than a car length apart) is a possible pass; when they're no longer alongside, if the car that started behind came out ahead, the share of the move it made with all four wheels below the line is the evidence (under half: benefit of the doubt). The line rule applies on superspeedways only, as in NASCAR (none of the presets is one; generated superspeedway ovals are). A restart is judged as a whole: the cars in the line when the pace car pulls off, and who is physically ahead of whom, are the reference; at the green, a car a car length clear of one that was ahead of it in the line got by before the line. Cars still catching up aren't lined up, so passing them isn't gaining a place.

**The caution order** works as it does for real. When the caution comes out the running order is frozen (race distance at that moment); it changes only when cars pit, retire, or are sent back. Lead-lap cars line up ahead of lapped cars, as in NASCAR: a car that pits rejoins at the back of its own lap's group (behind the lead-lap cars that stayed out, ahead of the lapped ones), and if the leader put a lap on it during its stop it's lapped. (A wave-around for lapped cars was tried and taken out: the cars going round to the tail left the field strung out at restarts.) Race control compares it with the cars on track, between cars that are in the line (within a row of their place): a car ahead of one it should be behind is told over the radio to line up behind it, and drives out of the way (up into the yield lane) to let it by; still out of order a full lap after the call (calls open at the green carry on) it's the stewards' case. "Ahead" is judged the shorter way round between the two cars, not by race distance: a car a lap down running ahead of a lead-lap car is ahead of it. Cars running ahead of the leader when the caution comes out aren't in the line; they go round and join its tail (waved around). Once the pace car is off, passing a car ahead in line before the green is a jumped restart. Getting here took a few wrong turns, kept in mind for the formation driving work to come: grace periods and distance windows hid the real problem; race distance mixes laps; a frozen order without a way to give places back deadlocks; waiting for the field to form up holds the race hostage to any stuck interaction.

**Formation driving** (the script that drives cautions for drivers without the caution license, and teaches the Cautions lesson):
- *Two phases*, as for real. *Caution out*: there's no line yet. Each driver slows to caution pace when they react, holds their lane and position, and closes up on the car ahead in their own way. *The pace car picks up the leader*: it waits at the end of pit road and pulls out just in front of the leader as it arrives; cars on the road between them are waved around. From there the line forms, and one to go comes no sooner than a full lap behind the pace car.
- *No hive mind*: every driver has their own reaction to the caution and the green (0.3-1 s), following gap and eagerness to close up, shifted by nerve (bold drivers sit closer and close up harder). Until they react they carry on: racing into a caution, holding station into a green.
- *Single file under caution* in the official order, in the middle of the track, a car length and the following gap apart; *doubled up at one to go*, the leader on the inside and the order alternating (the start's pace laps are double file throughout).
- *Giving way is one rule*: the car that's out of place moves low (a car's width inside the line) and slows; the car that belongs ahead carries on in the line. That covers a car told to line up behind another, one too far forward, and one being caught by a car that belongs ahead of it (it moves over before that car has to brake).
- *Catching up*: faster than the pace car, braking smoothly to the tail; a car may get by cars behind it in the official order (restoring the order), never cars ahead of it.
- *Passing a wreck*: on the side you're already on, never across in front of it.
- *The restart*: green when the leader crosses the line; everyone accelerates, but no car may gain a place until it has crossed the line itself (judged per car as it crosses, against cars in earlier rows that haven't crossed yet).
Out of order means clear ahead by a car length; nose to nose is two cars settling into line. Short track, 4 races: cars in their place at the green 58% -> 74%, average distance from their place at one to go 97 -> 44 m. Over 8 races, formation pileups on the short track went 2.3 -> 6.9 (single file, everyone braking at once) -> 1.3 (two phases).

**Road circuit rules** (`CIRCUIT_RULES`; a session races to the rulebook for its track type). The rulebook carries procedures as well as offences:
- *Standing start*: every car in its own grid box behind the line, staggered. The race joins the formation lap at its end (watchers needn't sit through the rest): each car 50 m short of its box, already staggered, rolling at formation pace (60% of the corner's limit, capped on straights) and braking gently into it; the field stops in its boxes and holds still on the brakes once the lights are on. Five red lights a second apart, a random 0.2-3 s hold, lights out: the race starts there, each driver launching on its own reaction (wheelspin and all), each car's first lap starting at the line. Waiting on the grid isn't stalling.
- *Safety car*: comes out of pit exit in front of the leader, as the pace car does, but drives the track at formation pace (quick on the straights, slow in the hairpins); the field stays single file throughout and restarts single file; each driver may drive the safety car's pace for where it is plus its own eagerness, so the line breathes like a real one (bunched in slow corners, stretched on straights) without anyone passing. Marshals with a crane lift a wreck off where it stopped (25 s); "safety car in this lap", into the pit entry, restart at the line.
- *Track limits*: every time the whole car is past the white line (its centre a car's half width beyond the edge), under green, counts once as it begins; pit lane and the pit approach don't. Race control counts them as they happen rather than the stewards reviewing each: up to the grace they're noted ("track limits at turn 4, 2 of 3"), the last with the black-and-white flag, and each one after that is 5 s. The grace (`trackLimits.strikes`, 3 here) is a league's to set: lenient for beginners, tight at the top. Corners are numbered from the start line, as on real circuits.
- Ovals keep the rolling start and the pace car (`OVAL_RULES`, no track limits: the apron has its own rule). Still to come for circuits: local yellows and the virtual safety car, and blue flags.
- *Valid laps*: any lap with a track-limits excursion is invalid. Its time doesn't count (best laps in the tower, results and records), though the car's place does. Road-course practice is scored on valid laps (an invalid lap's progress is thrown away), and the license needs a valid lap on each test circuit.

## Infield

Garage lot behind pit road (wrecks are parked there), escape lane from the back-straight apron across the infield. Progress only counts distance driven, so neither is a shortcut.

## Roster, events and records

Sessions run as **events**, each in its own worker. One event is on screen (attached): it runs in real time × the viewing speed and streams snapshots and tire-mark strokes to the renderer. The rest run in the background: races flat out (a 10-lap race finishes in a second or two), open sessions paused, since they exist to be watched and their learning happens in the trainer. The Events list (Watch view) lists them with Watch / Background / Stop. When a race finishes in the background, the weekend loop carries on in the background (practice, then the next race), so a season can run unwatched. **Whose drivers.** Paddock: *Drivers on track* switches between your own roster (it grows, learns, races the championship, saved in the browser) and a bundle shipped with the game, listed in `public/drivers/index.json`. *House drivers* are `public/drivers/default.json`, exactly as the benchmarks and Claude's tests race them: each car an exact copy, cycling through the bundle, with scripted crew chiefs and perfect spotters. A bundle is read-only (no learning, classes, practice or points) and never saved over your roster; `?roster=house` opens with it (handy for sharing what a test saw), and the choice is remembered. Only championship rounds score points: the field races the calendar's next track (Series: *Race the next round*, or the weekend loop). Under *Exhibition*, pick a venue, a series (only those that race on one of the venue's layouts can be picked; the rest are locked, the reason on hover), the layout and the session, then *Start*: an exhibition, recorded in everyone's history, no points.

Every agent (driver, spotter, crew chief) belongs to the organisation: seated in a team's car, or in the **academy** with no seat (released drivers land there; *Recruit* signs new ones). Whatever is on screen is an **event** (a race, a practice session, ghost laps) that claims its participants: an agent takes part in one event at a time, and may learn during any of them. Practice training runs only inside the practice session, so the field never practises and races at once.

Everything agents do goes into a permanent **activity log** (IndexedDB): arrivals, signings and releases, every practice round with best laps per track, licenses, crew chiefs' strategy rounds, race results (position, laps, best/average lap, stops, wall hits, points) and season standings, each tagged with the event and the agent's brain version. Click a name in the Paddock view's Roster (or double-click a driver in the timing tower) for their history page.

## Learning in races

On by default (*Learn* in the Series view's race settings). In a race each driver drives a small variation of itself for a lap and its opposite for another (which goes first is a coin toss, so tire wear and fuel burn can't fake a signal), and every three pairs steps toward the better direction. Only clean green-flag laps count: a lap with a caution, a pit stop or a wreck is run again with the same variation, and damage taken on a lap is charged 120 s per unit of health, so contact never pays. Learning is slow and steady (a few steps per driver per race; drivers improve their solo lap times ~0.5-1.5% over a dozen races in testing) and races stay as clean as without it. Each step reaches the roster as it happens and is logged in the driver's history; the HUD counts the steps taken this race.

**Fault.** Every car-to-car contact is judged on the velocities going in: closing on a car from behind is the follower's fault, moving across into a car is the mover's, and neither (touching side by side at the same speed) is shared. Each car keeps the damage it did to others times its share of the fault (`harmDone`), and the damage it did itself (walls, plus its share of contact: `selfInflicted`). Race learning charges a lap 120 s per unit of harm done, as for damage taken, and a lap the driver spoiled itself (it did itself or others harm) counts against its last clean lap time instead of being run again, even when it ends in a wreck: being wrecked by someone else still doesn't count. The caution and pit-traffic drills charge harm by fault too. The incidents benchmark reports contact retirements at fault vs hit by another, and the damage done by fault per race.

**Fear, trauma and nerve.** A real driver avoids risk for reasons that have nothing to do with the result: a big crash can end a career or a life. Drivers get some of that. *Fear* (`src/ai/fear.ts`) is a steady signal from moments of danger the driver makes itself: closing on the car ahead with under 1.2 s to contact, moving across into a car alongside or toward a wall with under 0.6 s. Crashes are rare, so learning from them alone is noise; near misses are constant. *Trauma* weighs each impact as the driver feels it: small knocks are nothing, big hits far worse than their size (`damage + 6·damage²`). Race learning charges a lap for seconds spent in danger and for trauma beyond the damage itself, scaled by the driver's *nerve*: its appetite for risk, 0 timid to 1 fearless (fear costs twice as much at 0, nothing at 1). Nerve is a personality trait: new drivers draw one, descendants inherit something of their mentor's, and the driver's page shows it. It doesn't change what a driver can do, only what it learns to care about. `npx tsx scripts/season.ts --nerve 0.5` runs a season with learning on (retirements, damage done, passes, lap time, danger per lap, race by race). First seasons: on the short track fear cut damage done to others race by race (16, 13, 8 against 22, 20, 19 without it) and passes by about 40%; nine races of race learning weren't enough to set it into the drivers, and it doesn't yet cut retirements.

**Injuries and careers.** A race's hardest single hit can hurt the driver, not just the car (`src/events/injury.ts`): nobody is hurt below 0.4 health lost in one hit; past that the chance rises (to 40% at most). Hits under 0.8 mean a minor injury (out 1-2 races), harder ones a serious one (3-6 races), and one in ten drivers hurt by a hit that destroys the car in one go never races again. Fault doesn't come into it. Injured drivers sit out races (counted in races run, since events only advance when run) while an academy driver stands in, and get their seat back once fit; a driver whose career is over keeps their history but races no more (no classes either). Injuries, recoveries and career endings are in the driver's history and the news. With today's drivers about two drivers in a 24-car race are hurt and a career ends every five or six races; cleaner drivers mean fewer. This is what makes nerve matter beyond one race's result.

**Racecraft (experimental).** A lesson for racing in a pack without wrecking anyone. What we learned getting it this far: drills scored only on crashes are noise (crashes are rare in 40 s), and pack drills alone let everything they don't measure drift. Drivers lost their way round the track, then got faster and hit harder, then got slower: one slow car in a 24-car field gets rear-ended all race. So drills are scored on fear too, every candidate also drives solo laps, and solo pace and cleanliness are held to the student's own from before the class (a moving reference let small losses through every round). Benchmarked after 80 rounds (retirements per 24-car race, against the bundled drivers): the speedway is clearly cleaner in every version (5.8 against 10.3 with the current one); the short track is worse (16 against 9.5), and the flat track is noisy. Part of the short track's count is the formation script's caution-line wrecks, which racecraft doesn't touch.

## Driving school

Pick a lesson and free drivers (academy or anyone not in a running session) in the Academy view's Driving school and start a class: it's an event like any other, on screen or in the background. Each round every student drives as a ghost alongside its slightly varied copies (32 in Rookie school, 16 in Track variety and Racecraft, 12 in Cautions) (faint dots in its colour; *Variants: Hide* to declutter) and then moves toward the copies that did better: the same evolution-strategies training as practice, one visible track per round. **Rookie school** rotates the three presets with soft walls hardening over the first 60 lessons; a student earns a license with a clean lap at full walls on each preset, and the class ends when everyone has one. **Track variety** puts licensed drivers on a new generated oval every round. Every round goes into each student's history (lesson, track, laps, best lap, how many variations beat them, wall softness). *Raw rookie* in the Paddock view's Roster signs an unlicensed driver for the academy.

## Drivers doing their own pit stops

**Withdrawn from the driving school (2026-10-16).** In the lab, both pit lessons cost drivers their racing: they train the whole network, and the outside guards didn't stop it (lap times 7-10 s slower, retirements doubled or worse). They come back as a separate pit-stop skill network (see PLAN.md); the lab can still run them. What follows describes how they worked.

The goal is no scripted driving: the pit maneuver and formation laps are scripts only until drivers learn them. Drivers see the pit stop in progress as network inputs (in-lap / out-lap, where the painted pit route runs at 0-100 m ahead, distance to the box, the speed limit zone and how far off the limit they are, the spotter's clear-to-merge), added with zero weights so racing is unchanged. The **Pit stops** lesson (Driving school, for licensed drivers) first has each student copy the scripted maneuver (DAgger: watch the script, then drive under its eye and learn its answers from wherever your own mistakes put you; a sample of the student's original racing is kept so learning to pit doesn't overwrite how it races), then practise: ghost pit stops scored on distance covered, with credit for getting onto pit road and slowing at the box before ever completing a stop, a drive-through's worth of penalty for speeding, and a penalty for not merging back. A clean stop on every preset earns the pit license, and from then on the driver drives its own stops in races. Students that stop making progress (no stop completed for a while) go back for a refresher with the script; three of the four bundled drivers earn the pit license within ~150 rounds.

The pit license is solo; races are not. **Pit road in traffic** (for drivers with a pit license) puts the stop in a race: two cars just ahead pit too (the veteran's maneuver), so there's a queue on pit road, and four more race around the student, two closing from behind as it slows. The racers drive as the student races, so it's a race's traffic. Students first go over the veteran's stop in traffic (DAgger, three starting lanes per preset), then practise; a student that settles into skipping the stop goes back for a refresher. The license takes a stop in traffic on every preset that is completed, unhurt, hurts nobody, never speeds, and scores at least as well as the veteran's maneuver driving the same drill. Drivers need both licenses to do their own stops in races. Two bundled drivers both earned it within 33 rounds (~5 minutes headless). Still scripted: pit stops for drivers without both licenses.

## The lab: training in the background

Training runs as a background job beside feature work (see `PLAN.md`). The lab is a second checkout of the repo (`../<repo>-lab`, made by `lab.ts init`) pinned to a commit, so classes and benchmarks there are never disturbed by edits here; `lab.ts sync` moves it to this checkout's HEAD (only committed code goes in). Experiments are JSON specs in `lab/queue/` (git ignores `lab/`): steps run in order, `class` (a driving school class, on 6 threads), `season` (races with learning on), `bench` (house vs candidate, 8 races a track by default, on 6 cores) and `promote`. `lab.ts run --once` works through the queue; each experiment gets a results folder (spec, step logs, the trained drivers, `bench.json`) and a line in `lab/RESULTS.md`. The **house drivers** (`lab/house.json`, seeded from the bundled ones) are what experiments start from; `promote` replaces them only if the benchmark says the candidate is no worse on any track (retirements, lap time) and better on at least one (retirements), keeping the old ones in `lab/house-history/`. Promoted house drivers reach the game by copying them to `public/drivers/default.json` in a commit.

## Lessons must not cost drivers their racing

Every lesson measures one skill, and a driver learning it is free to change everything else. Twice that went badly: Racecraft drivers drifted slower lap after lap, and a Cautions class licensed drivers that couldn't finish a solo lap (a field of them lost 23 of 24 cars a race). Three guards now apply to every drill lesson (`src/school/drill.ts`, `src/school/imitation.ts`):

- **Apprenticeships keep the racing in.** Each round trains on as many moments of the student's own original racing (solo laps on every preset) as of the skill being taught; caution moments are sampled rather than all recorded.
- **Practice holds solo pace.** Every round, each variation also drives 60 s of solo laps next to the student as it drove before the class, and is charged for distance and cleanliness lost against that (never rewarded for gaining). Distance, not laps completed: in 60 s most drivers complete exactly one lap, fast or slow, and a check on whole laps let 10% of pace through unseen.
- **Licenses check racing.** A license also needs clean solo laps within 3% of the pre-class pace on the track it is earned on, and a student who has passed stops learning in that lesson while the class catches up.

**The Cautions lesson trains only its own weights.** The guards above check racing from outside, and they missed traffic: solo laps stayed within 3% while racing in packs got worse (on the flat track, green-flag wrecks went from 8 to 21 in six races). The caution inputs are zero under green, so the lesson now trains only the first-layer weights from those inputs (`inputWeightMask`, in the apprenticeship and in practice, weight decay included): racing is untouched by construction, solo laps identical to the metre. The caution drills also come in two kinds: the field bunched after a start, and strung out round the lap (a race's cautions find cars half a lap from their place in line, and drivers trained only on bunched cautions charged the line). Place error is capped at 30 m a moment, so catching up fast isn't worth more than not hitting anyone. With all four bundled drivers driving their own cautions (only one licensed), the flat track is now within the noise of the formation script (7 retirements a race against 5; it was 15); the short track and speedway still lose more (15 and 17 against 12 and 11), with fewer cautions and less damage done by fault. Half of each practice pack is now classmates driving their own cautions (the other half the calm scripted pack), as in a race where everyone around a student drives theirs too; the license baseline uses the same pack. Three of four bundled drivers earned the license in 100 rounds (one in 60 before), and with only them driving their own cautions: speedway 13.5 and flat 6.6 retirements a race, within the noise of the formation script (10.9, 4.9), no formation pileups; the short track is still worse (15.4 against 11.6). The pit lessons can't use the same masking: trained on the pit inputs' weights alone, students mostly missed the pit road (unmasked, nearly all made clean stops), so they keep the outside guards.

## Drivers doing their own cautions

Same plan for formation laps, cautions and restarts. Drivers see a caution as more network inputs (pacing, restart coming, how far ahead or behind their place in the line is, which lane it's in, the line's speed, the gap and closing speed to the car ahead in their file), zero under green and added with zero weights. The **Cautions** lesson (for licensed drivers) runs drills in an 8-car field: a short green, a caution for debris, a caution lap, the restart, a short green. The rest of the pack is calm and scripted so the drill tests the student, not the pack. Each student first copies the formation script (DAgger, keeping a sample of its own green-flag racing), then practises: every variation drives its own drill from the same grid slot, scored on green-flag distance minus place and lane error under caution, passes under caution and damage done to others. The license takes a drill on every preset with no damage taken or done that scores at least as well as the formation script driving the same drill; from then on the driver drives its own cautions and restarts in races. Two bundled drivers both earned it within 45 rounds (~8 minutes headless). When the pace car isn't leading, the line follows a virtual pace car moving at pace speed from where the leader is (set from the leader itself, the line could wait on itself: cars ahead of their slots slowed for them, the leader stuck behind them slowed too, and the slots stopped). Under pacing, cars only swap places in line once one is clearly (8 m) ahead: re-sorting by distance every step swapped cars side by side in a row, and their files with them. (Freezing the order outright was worse: a car that really had got ahead had to drop back through a crawling pack, and the field gridlocked.)

## Rookie school

Brand-new drivers (random wiring, plus an instinct to accelerate and no attention to the radio yet) can't drive, and traffic practice teaches them nothing. Until they hold a license they practise solo as ghosts, with soft walls that harden over their own first 60 lessons, a damage penalty that starts gentle (so driving and crashing beats sitting still) and ramps to the race value over the next 60 lessons (learn to drive, then learn to drive clean), 16 variation pairs and 80 s trials, and they get first call on practice slots. They earn the license by lapping every track cleanly (90%+ health) at full damage, then practise in traffic like everyone else. Drivers descended from a trained mentor are licensed from the start. `npx tsx scripts/rookies.ts --field 8` checks how fast a fresh field graduates.
